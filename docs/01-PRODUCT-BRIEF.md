## 2. The project in one paragraph

**LitePack** turns a heavy video lesson into a tiny offline **study pack**: a timestamped transcript, the key board or slide images, an optional low-bitrate audio track, a plain-language summary, a glossary, and a short self-check quiz, all translated into the learner's language. A **teacher builds the pack once** on a laptop with a connection, using a Python command-line tool. The **learner opens it in a lightweight offline web app** (a PWA) on an old, shared phone and needs no data after the first download. Each pack is a single file, so it can also be passed from phone to phone.

**Pitch focus:** one barrier, "video lessons cost too much data and stop working when the signal drops." It mainly fits the **Connect** door and also covers **Understand** (transcript, translation, plain language) and **Interact** (screen reader, keyboard, adjustable text, audio).

## 3. Learner, task, barrier

The personas are **fictional composites**, as the rules require synthetic examples. Say so in the submission.

**Learner: Amina, 17.** A Form 3 student in a rural area of East Africa. She speaks Kiswahili at home and is taught in English. She shares her mother's older Android phone, which has Chrome and about 2 GB of free storage. She buys small prepaid data bundles, and the signal is 2G/3G and drops often.

**Task:** Her teacher assigns a 12-minute video on photosynthesis to watch before Thursday's class.

**Barrier:** Streaming the video costs ‹MEASURE: size of the demo video at 360p› MB of her bundle. It buffers on a weak signal and is gone when the signal drops. The English-only narration is hard to follow, and there is nothing to read or review afterwards without spending more data.

**Teacher: Mr. Otieno.** A secondary biology teacher with a laptop and school Wi-Fi a few times a week. He wants his students to get the lesson without the data bill.

### Evidence the barrier is real

Cite these in the Devpost story, and **re-check the numbers before submitting**.

- The Alliance for Affordable Internet's "1 for 2" target says 1 GB of mobile data should cost no more than 2% of average monthly income. ([Wikipedia: A4AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_for_Affordable_Internet))
- ITU (Nov 2025): the global median price of entry-level mobile broadband has fallen to about 1.4% of GNI per capita. That is a global figure, and low-income countries remain far above it. ([ITU Global Connectivity Report 2025, ch. 4](https://www.itu.int/itu-d/reports/statistics/2025/11/17/gcr-2025-chapter-4/))
- World Bank figures reported in 2026 put 1 GB in sub-Saharan Africa at about 2.4% of monthly income per capita, still above the target. ([Agence Ecofin](https://www.agenceecofin.com/actualites/2307-130350-en-afrique-subsaharienne-1-go-de-donnees-mobiles-represente-2-4-du-revenu-mensuel-par-habitant-bm))
- GSMA: about 38% of the world's population (about 3.1 billion people) live under mobile broadband coverage but don't use mobile internet. Affordability and skills are among the main reasons. ([Telefónica summary of GSMA SOMIC 2026](https://www.telefonica.com/en/communication-room/blog/coverage-usage-gsma-state-mobile-connectivity-2026/))
- GSMA Mobile Gender Gap Report 2025: in sub-Saharan Africa, women are 29% less likely than men to use mobile internet. ([GSMA press release](https://www.gsma.com/newsroom/press-release/progress-closing-the-mobile-internet-gender-gap-stalls-in-lmics-gsma-mobile-gender-gap-report-2025/))
- Streaming data use varies a lot by source. Published estimates for standard-definition YouTube range from roughly 150 MB to 560 MB per hour. **Do not quote these; measure the actual demo file instead** (03 · §7).

## 5. Scope

### MVP (must ship; the demo depends on all of these)
- **Teacher CLI:** video → transcript (Whisper, *or* import existing human captions) → key frames → pack `.zip` + size report + `.vtt` captions.
- **AI enrichment:** section titles, plain-language summary, key points, glossary, 5-question quiz.
- **Translation** into one target language (Kiswahili), with every item marked "machine-made" until reviewed.
- **Optional audio track** (Ogg Opus, 16 kbps mono) with transcript that follows along.
- **Learner PWA:** library, import a pack file, download from the hosted catalog, Lesson (images + transcript, language modes English / Kiswahili / both), Summary + glossary, Practice (quiz), Listen, Settings (text size).
- Works **fully offline** after the first visit plus one pack download.
- Accessibility baseline (02 §6.10).
- Hosted on GitHub Pages with **two demo packs**.

### P1 (should; build after MVP is done and deployed)
- **Teacher Studio:** review and edit translations and the quiz in the browser, mark them reviewed, re-export the pack.
- **Share a pack** through the OS share sheet (fallback: download the file).
- **Vision alt text:** a short image description plus transcription of the text on the board, for every frame.
- App UI translated into Kiswahili.
- Themes (dark, high contrast) and line-spacing setting.

### P2 (could; only if time remains)
- Single-file HTML export that works without installing the app.
- Offline machine translation with no API, using an open model (e.g. `Helsinki-NLP/opus-mt-en-sw`).
- More target languages; per-section plain-language rewrites.

### Out of scope (don't build)
User accounts, servers or backends, analytics/tracking, hosted video upload, SMS/feature phones, iOS-specific work, DRM, YouTube downloading.

### Cut line if behind schedule
Drop features in this order: Studio (P1) → vision alt text → UI translation → glossary. **Never cut:** offline support, the size report, the quiz, translation with machine badges, or accessibility.

## 6. Key decisions

| Decision | Choice | Why | Rejected alternative |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where processing runs | Teacher's laptop (Python CLI) | ffmpeg and Whisper are reliable natively; learner devices are weak and data is costly | In-browser ffmpeg.wasm/Whisper: tens of MB to download and slow on long videos |
| Learner app | Static PWA: **Preact + TypeScript + Vite** | Preact is a ~4 KB React-compatible runtime; installable; offline; free hosting | React (much larger runtime); native Android app (sideloading and store review) |
| Pack format | One `.litepack.zip`: `manifest.json` + WebP frames + optional Ogg Opus audio | One file is easy to share and inspect, and small | A folder of files; SQLite; base64-embedded HTML (P2 only) |
| On-device storage | IndexedDB stores **the pack zip + its parsed manifest**; unzip in memory when opened | One copy on disk; sharing sends the exact same file | Extracting every asset into separate records |
| Speech-to-text | **faster-whisper** `small`, int8 on CPU; prefer imported human captions when the source has them | Free, offline, good accuracy; CTranslate2 build is up to 4× faster than reference Whisper | Paid cloud speech-to-text |
| AI text tasks | Any **OpenAI-compatible** endpoint; default **Google Gemini API free tier** | Free quota; the same code works with Ollama, OpenRouter or OpenAI by changing 3 env vars | Lock-in to one provider SDK |
| Translation | Same LLM, ID-keyed JSON batches, then teacher review | Keys guarantee every line stays aligned; quality is reviewable | Argos Translate (Swahili model availability unverified) |
| Images | WebP, ≤ 800 px wide, grayscale when the frame has no colour | Lossy WebP is ~25–34% smaller than JPEG; supported by all target browsers | AVIF (slower to encode, patchier support on old Android) |
| Audio | Ogg Opus, mono, 16 kbps | Opus targets ~16–20 kbps for wideband speech; ~120 KB per minute | AAC/MP3 (larger at the same quality) |
| Routing | Hash routes (`#/pack/…`) | No server rewrite rules needed on GitHub Pages; robust offline | History API routes |
| Hosting | GitHub Pages via GitHub Actions | Free, HTTPS (required for service workers), easy for judges to review | Netlify/Vercel (fine fallbacks) |

## 7. Success criteria

Each must be **verified**, not assumed. 03 · §7 says how.

1. For each demo lesson, the pack without audio is **≤ 3%** of the source video's size, and **≤ 5%** with audio. (Measured.)
2. First visit transfers **≤ 150 KB** gzip for the app shell, with JavaScript **≤ 60 KB** gzip.
3. After one online visit and one pack download, **in airplane mode** the app opens and the Lesson, Summary, Practice and Listen screens all work.
4. A full lesson plus quiz can be completed **keyboard-only** and **with TalkBack**.
5. axe reports **0 serious or critical violations** on every route; Lighthouse Accessibility is ≥ 95 (target 100).
6. Malformed or unsafe pack files are **rejected with a clear message**, and the app doesn't crash.
7. **3+ real people** try it following the script in 03 · §8; findings and resulting changes are written down.
8. **Every machine-generated text** a learner sees is labelled as such until a human reviews it.

## 8. Content, licensing, privacy

- **Demo content** (two packs):
  - (a) A 5–8 minute slide lesson the team records itself. We own it, so it carries no licensing risk.
  - (b) One openly licensed video of 15 minutes or less, obtained through the **publisher's own download link**. For example, MIT OpenCourseWare offers downloads under CC BY-NC-SA and requires a link to its license with derivative works. Khan Academy content is also CC BY-NC-SA. **Do not use YouTube downloaders**; YouTube's terms restrict downloading.
- A pack built from CC BY-NC-SA material is a **derivative work**: keep the attribution, the same license and non-commercial use. The manifest's `source` block carries this, and the app shows it on every pack and on the About screen.
- **No real student data anywhere**, including tests, screenshots and the demo.
- **LLM privacy:** content sent to the unpaid Gemini tier may be used by Google to improve its products. Only send openly licensed lesson content, never personal data. `VERIFY` the current terms at <https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms>.
- **Translation review:** recruit at least one fluent Kiswahili reader to review the demo packs, and credit them with permission. If no Kiswahili reader is available, switch the target language to one a team member reads fluently (e.g. Hindi) and adjust the persona. Honest review matters more than the specific language.

## 10. Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pack | One `.litepack.zip` file holding one lesson |
| Manifest | `manifest.json` inside a pack; its format is `litepack/1` (02 §4) |
| Section | A time range of the lesson with one key frame and its transcript |
| Segment | One transcript line with start/end times |
| Key frame | A still image of the board or slide that represents a section |
| Catalog | `packs/index.json`, the list of packs hosted alongside the app |
| PWA | Progressive Web App: a website with a service worker that works offline and can be installed |
| Studio | The teacher review screen inside the app (P1) |
| Review status | `machine` (unchecked AI output) · `reviewed` (checked by a named person) · `human` (written by a person) |
